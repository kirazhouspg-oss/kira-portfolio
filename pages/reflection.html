Past Experiences and Expectations
My undergraduate degree was in Graphic Design, and I was first introduced to data visualization when I had to complete an information poster, in which I learnt to extract key information from complex datasets, and to organize and present it in a clear and concise way. I also developed the skills to use colour, graphics, and layout to effectively communicate information and make data more accessible and engaging.
Then I came across a book called PLAYFUL DATA (Shaoqiang, 2018), which features the work of a very wide range of talented designers who not only visualize data tangibly and memorably through interesting illustrations, but also inspire balancing colors, text, and images, as well as distinguishing between data and images when presented together with inspiration for primary and secondary information, and because of this book, I have developed a strong interest in data visualization.

Key Learnings
Giorgia Lupi's design concept for the Dear Data (2016) project focuses on the personalization of data visualization. Emphasizing the humanity of data, she uses hand-drawn illustrations to transform personal data into visual narratives, which are then shared as postcards between her and her collaborators. This approach emphasizes slow, thoughtful data processing over rapid digital quantification and aims to deepen human connection and understanding.

Incorporating Lupi's approach into my design practice allows for a greater focus on the storytelling of data. Emphasizing ‘context’ and personal engagement in my designs promotes a deeper connection with the audience, influences the viewer's perception of the data, and encourages greater reflection on the data presented.

For the Sound of Life project in Unit 2, I focused more on the narrative aspect of the project. I thought about how to extract information and ‘Show the data’ (Tufte, 2001).

Giorgia Lupi emphasizes the introduction of personal perspectives and feelings in data visualization (Lupi, 2016). I capture and display the sounds of my own living space, which in itself is a very subjective act. Because each person's perception and understanding of sound is unique, by carefully mapping the soundscape, I transform these sounds into a visual medium that demonstrates personal experiences and feelings. This subjectivity makes data visualization more than just a delivery of information, it is personal storytelling. And I want this project to demonstrate the multi-layered and multi-dimensional nature of sound. Different sounds have different frequencies, volumes, and durations, which are natural and intuitive to the user through an interactive format. I was able to demonstrate the interrelationships and variations of these different characteristics, providing a rich and deep soundscape.

For the project Variation Amounts of Decade Loans, I designed the charts with an eye to guiding viewers' reflections on changes in global lending data. By adding compelling lending data, I stimulated viewers to think about the long-term impact of COVID-19 on global lending trends and prompted them to think about what the future might hold. Combining this with Johanna Drucker's view, who emphasizes that graphic representations are not just tools for displaying information, but are the basis for shaping and communicating knowledge itself, Drucker argues for a critical understanding of graphics as interpretive acts that shape our understanding of information (Drucker, 2014). So I present the impact of COVID-19 on changes in global lending data by designing graphs that are more interpretive, narrative, and critical.

I was inspired by Lupi, and I also experimented with personalizing my data collection process by recording the fruit I ate each day in the form of a calendar. Photo documentation and collage were added to the project. Dancy (2018) Quantified Self Idea Volume is a way to gain insight into your life, habits, and behaviors by collecting, recording, and analyzing personal data. The amount of fruit intake per day not only calculates my daily Vitamin C (VC) intake but also reflects my personal preference for a particular fruit. I compare my Vitamin C intake over a month, analyze the trends over this time period, and think about the causes and effects, this introspection and comparative analysis helps to gain a deeper understanding of my eating habits and make adjustments. This personalized way of recording will make my data more emotional and unique.

Looking ahead: Shaping a creative practice


Based on the above understanding and learning about data visualization, my final project will explore smells, and among the myriad of smells that fill our olfactory system, the aroma of fruit is of particular interest. Fruit odours not only stimulate the senses but also have a profound effect on human physiology and psychology.

I will use data visualization to understand the effect of fruit odours on the human senses. It is estimated that 75% of human emotions are generated by the sense of smell (Herz, 2009). The profound effect of odour on human cognition can be summed up by the famous ‘Proustian Effect’, where just smelling a familiar odour can take a person back in time and evoke vivid memories and emotions (Proust, 1913).

Lupi emphasizes data personalization and sentimentality in her Dear Data (2016). In this project, I can learn from her approach to recording and visualizing fruit scent data in a personalized way. Allowing the audience to experience my personal emotions from my point of view. Drucker (2014) said We need to develop a domain of expertise focused on visual epistemology, and knowledge production in graphical form in fields that have rarely relied on visual communication. This encouraged me to think about how to translate fruit odour data into deeper knowledge and understanding. By exploring the stories and meanings behind the data, it is possible to make my visual charts more enlightening and meaningful, giving the audience a deeper understanding of the impact of fruit odours on the human senses.

Conclusion
This course has given me an in-depth understanding of data visualization, which I have come to understand more thoroughly through a combination of critical reading, writing, and creative practice. This has laid the foundation for my future career, I will use a range of media, tools, and techniques, and I will research, interpret, critique, and visualize data developing creative narratives that a wide variety of audiences can understand.
